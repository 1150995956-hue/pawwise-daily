Why Do Cats Scratch Furniture?

A scratched sofa is frustrating, but scratching is a normal cat behaviour—not spite or revenge. Cats scratch to maintain their claws, stretch, leave scent and visual marks, and express excitement. The goal is not to stop scratching altogether. It is to make the right places more appealing.

The simple plan: Put a sturdy scratching post right beside the furniture your cat targets, choose a texture and orientation your cat likes, reward use, and temporarily protect the furniture. Keep the approved scratching spot available every day.

Why cats scratch

Scratching is part of normal feline behaviour. It helps loosen and remove the outer sheath of the claws, gives the body a satisfying stretch, and leaves scent and visible marks. Cats may scratch after waking, during play, when excited, or near places where people and pets move through the home. A favourite sofa corner may simply have the right texture, height, location, or stability.

Choose a scratching post your cat will actually use

  • Make it stable. A post that wobbles or tips can make a cat avoid it. Choose a heavy base or secure it safely.
  • Match the direction. If your cat stretches up a sofa arm, try a tall vertical post. If they scratch rugs, offer a horizontal or angled scratcher.
  • Try different textures. Sisal, corrugated cardboard, and sturdy carpet-like materials feel different. Offer a choice and observe what your cat prefers.
  • Make it long enough. A vertical post should allow a comfortable full-body stretch.
  • Put it in the right place. Start beside the target furniture, near a sleeping spot, or in a well-used room—not hidden away in a rarely visited corner.

How to redirect scratching away from furniture

  1. Place the new scratcher directly beside the area your cat scratches. If necessary, add another scratcher at a second favourite location.
  2. Make the furniture less rewarding temporarily with a purpose-made, pet-safe furniture protector. Check it often and follow the product instructions.
  3. When your cat uses the scratcher, offer calm praise, play, or a small treat if appropriate. You can use a toy to invite exploration, but never force the paws onto the post.
  4. Keep the scratcher clean and stable. Replace worn cardboard or damaged posts before they become unattractive or unsafe.
  5. Once the scratcher is used reliably, move it gradually if you need it in a different location—just a little at a time.
Helpful tip: Cats often scratch after waking. Placing a scratcher near a favourite sleeping place can make the appropriate choice convenient at exactly the right moment.

What not to do

  • Do not yell, spray water, hit, or frighten your cat. Punishment can create fear and does not teach which surface to use.
  • Do not declaw as a furniture solution. Declawing is an amputation procedure, not a nail trim; discuss humane claw-care and behaviour options with a veterinarian.
  • Do not use sticky materials, scents, or deterrent products unless they are specifically labelled safe for cats and used as directed.
  • Do not remove every scratching option. Scratching is a normal need; provide suitable alternatives.

When to ask for help

If scratching behaviour changes suddenly, your cat seems distressed, damages their claws, avoids using a paw, or has skin or nail problems, arrange a veterinary check. If your cat scratches people or other pets, or the behaviour is causing serious household stress, a veterinarian or qualified feline behaviour professional can help you build a safe plan.

Quick home checklist

  • At least one stable scratcher near the furniture being targeted.
  • More than one texture or orientation if you are unsure what your cat prefers.
  • Daily play and predictable opportunities to climb, explore, and rest.
  • Positive rewards for using approved scratching surfaces.
  • Regular checks of claws, paws, and scratcher condition.
