How to Choose a Cat Scratching Post

The right scratching surface can protect your furniture and give your cat a healthy place to stretch, scratch and mark their territory. Here is what to look for before you buy.

Choose a scratching area that fits your cat's natural habits and your home's layout.

Quick answer: Start with a sturdy post that allows a full-body stretch, or a horizontal cardboard scratcher if your cat prefers floor-level surfaces. Stability and your cat's preference matter more than appearance.

Why the right scratcher matters

Scratching is normal cat behaviour. Cats use it to maintain their claws, stretch their bodies and leave scent and visual marks. A scratching post is not simply a toy: it gives your cat an appropriate surface for an instinctive activity.

If your cat scratches a sofa, the goal is not to punish the behaviour. It is to make an acceptable scratching surface more appealing and easier to use. For practical ways to redirect the habit, read our guide to why cats scratch furniture.

1. Choose the right shape for your cat

Vertical posts

A vertical post is a good starting point for cats that stand up and stretch when scratching. Look for enough height for a long stretch, plus a broad, secure base. A narrow post that rocks every time your cat uses it may be ignored.

Horizontal scratchers

Flat cardboard or sisal boards suit cats that scratch rugs, mats or other floor-level surfaces. They can also fit easily in smaller rooms. Check that the board does not slide around during use.

Angled or ramp-style scratchers

An angled surface offers a middle option for cats that like to scratch at a slope. It may be useful when your cat ignores both a straight post and a flat board.

Multi-level cat trees

A cat tree can combine scratching, climbing and resting. Choose one with a stable footprint and platforms appropriate for your cat's size and mobility. More features are not automatically better if the structure wobbles.

There is no universal winner. If possible, notice what your cat already scratches: vertical upholstery, carpet, cardboard packaging or a rough mat. Use that clue to choose the first surface to try.

3. Check height, base and build quality

  • Enough height: A vertical post should let your cat extend its body comfortably.
  • A wide, heavy base: The post should stay upright when your cat leans into it.
  • Secure materials: Avoid loose cords, exposed staples, sharp edges and pieces that can be chewed off and swallowed.
  • Appropriate size: A large or energetic cat may need a sturdier, larger scratcher than a small kitten.
  • Easy upkeep: Replace damaged cardboard and repair or replace worn surfaces before they become unsafe.
Safety note: Stop using a scratcher if it becomes unstable, has sharp broken parts, or sheds pieces that your cat may swallow. For kittens, older cats or cats with mobility concerns, choose a design that is easy to approach and use.

4. Put the scratcher where your cat wants to use it

Even a well-made post can be ignored if it is hidden in an unused corner. Place a new scratcher near the sofa, chair or doorway your cat already targets. Many cats also like a scratching surface near a favourite sleeping area because stretching and scratching often happen after waking.

If your cat uses the new surface, reward the choice with calm praise, play or a small treat if appropriate. You can make the old target less appealing temporarily while keeping the new scratcher close by. Avoid shouting, spraying water or physically forcing your cat's paws onto the post.

5. Buying mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a post only because it matches the room, without checking whether it is stable.
  • Buying a short vertical post that does not allow a full stretch.
  • Assuming every cat likes sisal; some prefer cardboard or another texture.
  • Putting the scratcher far away from the place where scratching already happens.
  • Buying a complex cat tree when a simple, sturdy board would better match your cat's habits.

Frequently asked questions

How tall should a cat scratching post be?

Choose a post that lets your cat stretch close to full length without making it wobble. Larger cats usually need a taller and sturdier design.

Is sisal or cardboard better?

Neither is best for every cat. Sisal suits many cats that prefer a firm vertical surface; corrugated cardboard often appeals to cats that scratch horizontally. Your cat's preference is the best guide.

Where should I put a scratching post?

Start near the furniture or doorway your cat already scratches. You can add another near a favourite sleeping spot if your home has room.

How many scratchers does a cat need?

Offer more than one suitable scratching location when possible, especially in a multi-room home or a home with several cats. Put them in places your cat naturally spends time.

Final checklist before you buy

Choose a shape that matches your cat's habits, a texture your cat may enjoy, a stable base, a safe finish and a location near an existing scratching spot. If your first choice is ignored, try a different orientation or material rather than assuming your cat cannot learn to use a scratcher.

This guide is general educational information, not veterinary advice. If scratching behaviour changes suddenly or comes with signs of pain, ask a veterinarian.
