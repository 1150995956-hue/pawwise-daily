How to set up a cat-friendly litter box

A clean, accessible litter box in a calm location can make everyday life easier for both cats and people. Small details—such as box size, litter texture and placement—can matter.

1. Start with enough boxes

A commonly recommended starting point is one litter box per cat, plus one extra. In a multi-cat home, place boxes in more than one location rather than lining them all up together. This gives cats options and can reduce competition.

2. Choose a box your cat can use comfortably

Many cats prefer a roomy, simple, uncovered box. A large cat may need a larger tray, while a kitten, senior cat or cat with mobility difficulty may need a low entry. If you are unsure what your cat prefers, offer a suitable alternative and observe which one is used.

3. Consider unscented litter

Many cats prefer unscented, finer-textured litter. If you are changing litter, do it gradually rather than making a sudden switch. Avoid strong fragrances intended to mask odors; a clean box and good placement are usually a better starting point.

4. Pick a quiet, accessible location

Choose a calm spot that your cat can reach at all times. Keep litter boxes away from food and water bowls, noisy appliances and places where another pet could block the exit. Older cats or cats with mobility problems may need a box on each floor they use.

5. Keep the box clean

Remove waste at least daily and refresh the litter as needed. Wash the tray periodically with a gentle, unscented cleaner, rinse it well and dry it before refilling. Replace trays that are cracked or retain odors that cannot be removed.

6. If your cat stops using the box, look for a cause

Do not assume it is misbehavior. The box may be dirty, difficult to reach, in an uncomfortable location, or filled with litter your cat dislikes. Conflict with another pet can also make a box feel unsafe. A sudden change in toileting can also be related to pain or illness.

Important health warning: Straining to urinate, repeated trips to the box with little or no urine, crying while trying to urinate, or an inability to pass urine can signal an emergency—especially in male cats. Seek urgent veterinary care. Any new or persistent litter-box change deserves a call to your veterinarian.

A quick litter-box checklist

  • Enough boxes, placed in separate quiet locations.
  • A roomy box with an entry your cat can manage.
  • Unscented litter if your cat prefers it.
  • Waste removed daily and trays kept clean.
  • Food and water kept separate from litter areas.
  • Sudden toileting changes discussed with a veterinarian.
