Dog care basics: building a healthy daily routine

A good dog-care routine is not complicated, but it should be consistent. Start with the essentials below, then adjust them to your dog’s age, size, health, and temperament.

Updated November 19, 2026 · Pawwise Daily Editorial Team. This educational guide is not individually veterinary reviewed and does not replace advice from your veterinarian.

1. Choose food for your dog’s life stage

Look for a complete and balanced dog food appropriate for your dog’s life stage. Puppies, adults, and seniors may have different nutritional needs. Follow feeding guidance on the package as a starting point, measure portions, and monitor body condition. Ask your veterinarian before making major diet changes or adding supplements.

2. Keep fresh water available

Provide clean drinking water throughout the day. Wash bowls regularly and check that water is available after exercise and in warm weather. A sudden change in thirst can be worth discussing with a veterinarian.

3. Make exercise and enrichment part of the day

Walks, sniffing opportunities, safe games, and reward-based training can support physical and mental wellbeing. The right amount depends on health, age, breed, and fitness. Build activity gradually and avoid pushing a dog that seems tired, sore, or distressed.

4. Groom gently and check regularly

Brush according to coat type, keep nails at a comfortable length, and check ears, paws, skin, and coat for changes. Introduce grooming gradually with rewards. Ask a veterinarian or qualified groomer for help if your dog is uncomfortable or you are unsure how to trim nails safely.

5. Use kind, consistent training

Reward the behaviours you want to see. Keep sessions short, clear, and positive. Avoid punishment-based methods that may increase fear. If behaviour changes suddenly or your dog shows aggression or severe anxiety, seek help from a veterinarian or qualified humane trainer.

6. Protect health with preventive care

Schedule routine veterinary checkups and discuss vaccinations, parasite prevention, dental care, and microchipping where appropriate. Keep medicines and toxic foods or household products out of reach.

7. Make home a safe place

Provide a quiet resting area, secure doors and fences, and remove hazards such as accessible chemicals, medications, small swallowable objects, and unsafe plants. Use a properly fitted harness or collar and identification when outside.

When to get help: Trouble breathing, collapse, seizures, severe bleeding, repeated unproductive retching, or suspected poisoning need urgent veterinary attention. When in doubt, call a veterinary clinic for guidance.

A simple daily checklist

  • Fresh water and appropriate meals.
  • Toilet breaks, movement, and sniffing time.
  • Play, training, or another enrichment activity.
  • A quick look at appetite, energy, movement, skin, and behaviour.
  • A comfortable, clean place to rest.

This guide is general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Your veterinarian can tailor advice to your individual dog.
