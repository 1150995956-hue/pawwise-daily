How to encourage your cat to drink more water

Published November 21, 2026 · Pawwise Daily Editorial Team. This is general educational information, not an individually veterinary-reviewed article or a substitute for advice from your veterinarian.

Some cats drink in small amounts throughout the day, and it can be difficult to tell how much they are taking in. Rather than forcing your cat to drink, make fresh water easy to find and pleasant to approach. Small changes to the bowl, location and feeding routine may help.

1. Offer more than one water station

Place water in several quiet, easy-to-reach spots. In a multi-level home, consider having a station on each floor. Keep bowls away from litter trays and busy areas where your cat may feel disturbed. In a home with several pets, provide enough separate stations to reduce competition.

2. Try a different bowl shape and material

Cats can have individual preferences. Try a wide, shallow bowl that lets your cat approach from different angles. Ceramic, stainless steel and glass are practical options when they are sturdy and easy to clean. Avoid bowls with cracks, chipped surfaces or strong lingering odours. Place the bowl on a washable mat to keep the area tidy.

3. Keep water fresh and bowls clean

Refresh the water regularly and wash bowls every day with dish soap, rinse them thoroughly and dry them before refilling. A bowl that looks clean can still develop a slippery film. If your cat stops using a bowl, clean it and try moving it to a quieter location.

4. Experiment with a drinking fountain

Some cats are interested in moving water, while others prefer a still bowl. If you try a fountain, choose one that is easy to take apart and clean. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning schedule, replace filters as directed and keep a regular water bowl available as an alternative. A fountain is not essential, and it should not be left uncleaned.

5. Ask whether wet food is suitable

Wet food contains more moisture than dry food and may contribute to a cat's overall water intake. If you want to change your cat's diet, choose a complete and balanced food appropriate for their life stage and introduce it gradually. Cats with medical conditions or prescribed diets need an individual plan from their veterinarian. Do not add salt, broth, milk or flavourings to water unless your veterinarian confirms the ingredient is safe for your cat.

6. Make the water area comfortable

Keep bowls away from loud appliances, narrow passageways and places where another animal can block access. Some cats prefer a little distance between their food and water. You can test different arrangements and observe which station your cat uses, rather than assuming every cat has the same preference.

7. Monitor changes instead of guessing

Notice whether the water level changes, whether your cat visits the bowl and whether urine clumps in the litter tray appear different from usual. These observations are not a diagnosis, but they can help you describe changes to your veterinarian. In a multi-cat home, it may be difficult to identify which cat is drinking; separate stations can make observation easier.

When to contact a veterinarian: A sudden increase or decrease in drinking, changes in urination, weight loss, vomiting, reduced appetite or unusual tiredness deserve veterinary advice. If your cat is straining to urinate, repeatedly visiting the litter tray with little or no urine, crying in the tray or unable to pass urine, seek emergency veterinary care immediately. Do not wait to see whether a water-bowl change fixes the problem.

A simple water-station checklist

  • Fresh water is available in more than one accessible place.
  • Bowls are clean, stable and easy for the cat to approach.
  • Water is away from litter trays and high-traffic areas.
  • A fountain, if used, is cleaned and maintained as directed.
  • Any sudden change in thirst or urination is discussed with a veterinarian.
