Cat care basics: a comfortable, confident home

Cats thrive when their basic needs are met and they can choose when to play, rest, explore, and interact. Use this guide as a starting point and adapt it to your cat.

Updated November 19, 2026 · Pawwise Daily Editorial Team. This educational guide is not individually veterinary reviewed and does not replace advice from your veterinarian.

1. Feed a complete, age-appropriate diet

Choose a nutritionally complete cat food suitable for your cat’s life stage. Follow the feeding guide as a starting point, measure portions, and monitor body condition. Cats have specific nutritional needs; do not replace their regular diet with dog food or homemade recipes unless a veterinary nutrition professional has formulated the plan.

2. Make fresh water easy to reach

Provide clean water every day and wash bowls regularly. Some cats prefer wide bowls or a water fountain. Place water away from litter trays and, if practical, offer more than one drinking location.

3. Keep litter areas clean and accessible

Scoop litter regularly and keep trays in quiet, easy-to-reach places. Many cats prefer an uncovered tray and unscented litter. A useful starting point is one tray per cat plus one extra, adjusted for your home. Sudden changes in litter habits can signal stress or illness and should be discussed with a veterinarian.

4. Offer play and places to climb

Short interactive play sessions with safe wand toys can encourage movement and satisfy hunting instincts. Provide scratching surfaces, hiding places, and elevated resting spots. Rotate toys to keep the environment interesting, and put away strings or small objects that could be swallowed.

5. Groom gently

Brush according to coat length and your cat’s tolerance. Check for mats, skin changes, and overgrown nails. Build handling slowly with treats and breaks. Never force a grooming session if your cat is distressed; ask a veterinarian or experienced groomer for help when needed.

6. Make the home safe

Keep medications, cleaning products, string, and small swallowable objects out of reach. Many common plants—including lilies—can be extremely dangerous to cats. Check plant safety before bringing greenery indoors, secure windows and balconies, and provide a quiet place where your cat can retreat.

7. Stay on top of preventive health

Arrange routine veterinary checkups and ask about vaccinations, parasite prevention, dental care, microchipping, and spaying or neutering. Cats can hide discomfort, so changes in appetite, drinking, urination, mobility, grooming, or behaviour deserve attention.

Urgent warning: A cat that is straining to urinate, especially a male cat, needs urgent veterinary assessment. Trouble breathing, collapse, seizures, suspected poisoning, or inability to pass urine also require emergency care.

A simple daily checklist

  • Fresh food and water appropriate for your cat.
  • Clean litter trays and a quick check of normal toileting.
  • Play, exploration, scratching, and safe resting places.
  • A gentle check for changes in appetite, energy, movement, or behaviour.
  • Calm attention on your cat’s terms.

This article is general education, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Contact a licensed veterinarian for individual advice.
